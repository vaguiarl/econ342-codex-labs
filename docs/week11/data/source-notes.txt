SUEZ: RECONSTRUCTION OF FEYRER'S FIGURE 1
Primary source: Feyrer (2021), Journal of Development Economics.
Author article and replication links:
https://economics.dartmouth.edu/people/james-feyrer

We use the author's replication archive, the July 16, 2021 do-file, and working
bilateral dataset (Suez_bilat_trade.dta). This is a reconstruction from author
working data, not a new raw-data build or a replication of the regression tables.

The file feyrer_figure1_group_year_means.csv contains 108 group-year observations:
27 years (1958–1984) x four distance groups. The balanced panel contains 34,938
observations and 1,294 pairs. The author's sample flags and combatant exclusions
are retained; excluded countries are ISR, EGY, SYR, JOR, SDN, LBN, LBY, and TUR.
The upstream sample and US/Canada east/west port splits are inherited from the
working data. Group pair counts are 1,060; 155; 55; 24.

For each pair-year, adjusted log trade is:
  ln_trade - pair mean of ln_trade - year mean of ln_trade + overall mean.
In this complete balanced panel, that is the residual from a regression on
pair and year fixed effects. Average these residuals within each year and
sea-distance group. This removes fixed pair differences and common year shocks,
not pair-specific trends. The exported mean_ln_trade_residual column supplies
the plotted line. Both sets of residual means are below 2.7e-15 in absolute value.

Reverse the sign of the author's suez_change and calculate exp(suez_change)-1.
The author's float storage is matched before applying strict thresholds >0.1,
>0.5, and >1. Groups are 0–10%, >10–50%, >50–100%, and >100% route increases.
Annual points are connected by straight lines; no smoothing or invented annual
observations are used. Event lines are 1967.417 and 1975.417, as in the do-file.
The difference from emulating the author's final Stata float storage is at most
1.36e-8 log points. Double precision is retained in this export.

Zero is an adjusted baseline, not zero trade. Lower values mean weaker trade
after adjustment. These paths are descriptive group averages, not four causal
coefficients. Pre-closure trends also differ. The design needs the comparison
that other shocks do not track route exposure. This transport-channel evidence
does not establish whether trade prevents war.

COLOMBIA: TRANSFORMATION OF PUBLISHED ESTIMATES
Primary source: Dube and Vargas (2013), Commodity Price Shocks and Civil Conflict:
Evidence from Colombia, Review of Economic Studies. Published author-hosted PDF:
https://odube.net/papers/Dube_commodities_conflict.pdf
Exact numerical sources: Table 2, column 2, and printed page 1403.

The file dube_vargas_table2_paramilitary_transformation.csv contains published
coefficients and standard errors plus our arithmetic transformation. No
regression was independently re-estimated.

Input                          Coefficient   Department-clustered SE   Exposure
Domestic coffee price          -0.160        0.061                     1.52
World oil price                 0.726        0.156                     0.083
Coffee exposure: thousand hectares. Oil: hundred thousand barrels per day.
Exposure is the rounded published mean among positive-producing municipalities.

For a 10% price rise, the expected differential annual paramilitary attacks per
100 equally exposed municipalities, relative to municipalities with zero
exposure, are:
  effect = 100 x coefficient x mean positive exposure x ln(1.10)
  transformed SE = 100 x clustered SE x mean positive exposure x ln(1.10)
  approximate normal 95% interval = effect +/- 1.96 x transformed SE
The log change is 0.0953101798043, not 0.10.

Coffee: -2.31794357284; interval [-4.05002690765, -0.585860238036].
Oil:     0.574320081465; interval [0.332441144676, 0.816199018254].

The per-100 unit is a rescaling of the annual per-municipality outcome. Fractional
counts describe expected averages. Dots are estimates; bars show uncertainty
under the statistical and identification assumptions. Exposure is held fixed;
intervals do not include uncertainty in the rounded exposure means. They use a
normal critical value and the published clustered standard errors.

Compare directions, not an intrinsic ranking of coffee versus oil effects:
production units and typical exposures differ. Opposite signs are consistent
with opportunity-cost and revenue-capture mechanisms; the chart does not by
itself establish those mechanisms or an effect for Canada.

ATLANTIC DESIGN: VERSION USED IN THE CLASS
Ahsan, Panza and Song, CEPR DP14206, revised March 2020, Tables 4–5:
https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/cepr-dp14206_ada-ns.pdf
Later publication, Economic Journal (2025):
https://doi.org/10.1093/ej/ueae102

The class uses the accessible March 2020 design and qualitative finding. The
historical data cover 1640–1896; main IV estimates restrict to the sailing era
before 1871. The course does not quote a final-2025 numerical estimate.
Cyclone shocks interacted with wind-based sailing time shift Atlantic market
integration measured using wheat-price transmission. Causal interpretation
requires that this variation affect European conflict only through trade.
Fleet destruction is a plausible alternative military-power channel, even for
land conflict; this is a concern to examine, not a demonstrated invalidation.

REUSE AND VERIFICATION
These teaching transformations retain attribution to the original sources.
Original source data remain subject to their source terms. CSVs allow the chart

contains processed chart values rather than redistribution of full source
archives or articles.

PANAMA CONSTRUCTION AND OPENING: ROUTE ARITHMETIC AND PUBLISHED EVIDENCE
Added 2026-11-23. No new causal estimates or synthetic DiD were run.

Historical route source: Marshall (1913), map on p. 226, University of South
Florida collection:
https://etc.usf.edu/maps/pages/10400/10479/10479.htm

The file historical_route_comparisons.csv contains selected distances from the
1913 comparison, in nautical miles. All routes start in New York:
Destination       Alternative route     Alternative miles   Panama miles
San Francisco     Strait of Magellan     13,135              5,262
Yokohama          Suez                   13,079              9,798
Manila            Suez                   11,589              11,548

Saved distance = alternative distance - Panama distance.
Percentage reduction = 100 x saved distance / alternative distance.
The results are 59.939094%, 25.086016%, and 0.353784%, respectively, displayed
as 59.9%, 25.1%, and 0.4%. These are route arithmetic from historical map
values, not observed freight-cost savings, trade effects, or causal estimates.
A 60% distance saving need not imply a 60% freight-cost saving. Tolls,
congestion, inland connections, destination size, and firms' responses matter.
Construction began under US administration in 1904; commercial passage opened
in August 1914. Canal Authority opening chronology:
https://pancanal.com/en/history-in-pictures-107-years-of-the-panama-canal/

Final published research: Stephan Maurer and Ferdinand Rauch (2023), Economic
geography aspects of the Panama Canal, Oxford Economic Papers 75(1), 142–162.
Online publication: 25 March 2022. DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1093/oep/gpac009

Author-reported published Table 3, column 3: preferred coefficient 2.303,
clustered standard error 1.058, 12,125 observations and 47 spatial clusters.
The outcome is log county population. Exposure is the log change in total
market potential, interacted with the 1940 indicator. The panel years are
1880, 1900, 1910, 1920, and 1940. County/year effects and year-specific effects
of longitude, latitude, 1880 log population, and coastal status are included.

Interpretation: approximately 2.3% greater population by 1940 per 1%
improvement in market potential, conditional on the identification assumptions.
This is continuous-exposure DiD, not synthetic DiD. It is not an annual growth
rate, trade-flow elasticity, or estimate of aggregate welfare gains.

Published Table 6, Panel D adds state x year effects (despite the short heading
'State FE'): coefficient -0.342, standard error 1.403. The positive, precise
baseline result does not survive this specification. Much exposure varies
across states, so other regional changes are difficult to separate from canal
access. Predicted access gains must not proxy for other differential population
shocks. World War I, anticipation, regional development, and spillovers remain
relevant. Population can also move between regions.

The classroom synthetic-DiD extension is proposed, not estimated: weight
lower-exposure places to resemble higher-exposure places before the opening,
then compare changes. Few pre-opening observations are available, and 1910 is
already during construction. Lower exposure is not necessarily zero exposure.
A good pre-opening match does not establish that the comparison is unaffected
by war, anticipation, or spillovers.

Published table text and notes were source-audited; no local final paper PDF,
replication files, independently verified research dataset, or fresh estimates
were obtained. The route chart is a separate teaching calculation. The 2019
working-paper estimate and its pre-period description are not used here.
