Choose one

Two consequential Canadian decisions

Both options ask whether a larger economic scale improves welfare once political, spatial, distributional, and capacity constraints are taken seriously.

Option A

The Size of Nations

Decision: Which scale benefits can Canada obtain through deeper economic integration while retaining political autonomy?

Mental model

A larger market can spread fixed costs and support variety. A larger political jurisdiction may also share public-good costs, but common policy fits less well when preferred taxes, regulation, redistribution, or services differ. Trade can make market size different from country size.

Required comparison

  • Status quo: sovereign Canada with CUSMA market access.
  • Deeper integration while national governments remain.
  • Hypothetical Canada–U.S. political integration.

Useful evidence

  • Country size and openness, with Canada and pre-selected peers highlighted.
  • U.S. shares of Canadian merchandise exports and imports over time.
  • Comparable policy-preference evidence or carefully labelled institutional proxies.
Primary-source starter pack
  • Alesina and Spolaore: On the Number and Size of Nations
  • Alesina, Spolaore, and Wacziarg: Economic Integration and Political Disintegration
  • World Bank Indicators API
  • Statistics Canada Table 12-10-0171-01
  • CUSMA economic impact assessment

Option B

100 Million Canadians

Decision: Under what spatial and policy conditions could a Canada of 100 million raise living standards per person rather than merely total GDP?

Mental model

Population can enlarge the domestic market. Urban concentration can also let firms and workers share specialized inputs, match in thicker labour markets, and learn from nearby activity. Housing scarcity, congestion, infrastructure pressure, and unequal access can offset those gains.

Required comparison

  • Concentrated growth in today’s largest metropolitan areas and corridors.
  • Polycentric growth across more mid-sized and emerging urban centres.
  • Both spatial allocations must sum to 100 million.

Useful evidence

  • Official population scenarios versus a transparent path to 100 million.
  • A map or table allocating population across provinces or CMAs.
  • Productivity per person alongside housing, commuting, or infrastructure capacity.

The writing discipline

Each page has one job

This is a course adaptation of an Amazon-style narrative memo: answer first, reason in complete paragraphs, use evidence inside the argument, and make the document readable before any discussion begins.

Decision and answer

Name the decision-maker, baseline, and counterfactual. Give the answer and its two or three decisive conditions in the first substantive paragraph.

Economic mechanism

Explain the beneficial force and the countervailing force in ordinary language. Define concepts before using technical terms.

Evidence and limits

Present one baseline exhibit and one comparison or counterfactual. Explain what each establishes, what it cannot establish, and why it changes the judgment.

Tradeoffs and verdict

Address the strongest objection, distribution, uncertainty, measurable conditions, and the observation that would make you reverse the recommendation.

One-week Codex workflow

Use AI like a research assistant

AI may map claims, audit data, debug code, and red-team the argument. It is never evidence and cannot certify its own output.

1 · Work backward

Define the decision, tentative verdict, mechanisms, needed evidence, and possible refutations.

2 · Acquire safely

Use a dedicated project folder. Preserve downloaded files unchanged in data/raw.

3 · Audit definitions

Build a data dictionary. Check units, years, geography, denominators, missingness, and price concepts.

4 · Build benchmarks

Generate the exhibits from code. Assert totals and endpoints; manually verify three observations.

5 · Draft answer first

Write the verdict before background. Connect every empirical paragraph to the decision.

6 · Red-team

Ask Codex to flag unsupported claims, causal overreach, inconsistent benchmarks, and a weak counterargument.

7 · Reproduce

Run from raw data in a clean folder, regenerate the figures, test PDF links, and disclose AI corrections.

Quality control

The project must survive an audit

A high-scoring memo does not need a dramatic conclusion. It needs a conclusion whose confidence matches the evidence.

Verification gate

  • Every number traces to a named dataset, variable, and raw row.
  • Years, currencies, prices, units, geographies, and denominators are consistent or disclosed.
  • At least three observations were checked manually.
  • Raw data remain unchanged and missing values or exclusions are reported.
  • Every exhibit regenerates from a clean run.
  • A sensitivity check tests whether the recommendation changes.
  • The AI log records retained output, important errors, and corrections.
