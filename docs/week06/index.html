The Krugman model in one paragraph

Mental model

People value different varieties of the same broad good. Producing each variety requires a fixed cost. Because varieties are imperfect substitutes, each firm has some pricing power, while competition and entry limit profit. When trade joins markets, countries can specialize in different varieties rather than entire industries. Each country exports some varieties, imports others, and consumers gain access to more choice.

The unit of specialization is often a model or variety, not the entire industry.

Example Name two products that count as different auto varieties rather than different industries.

Monopolistic competition in ordinary language

Why “monopolistic”?

A firm is the only producer of its exact variety. A Civic is not identical to a Corolla, so its producer faces downward-sloping demand and has limited price-setting power.

Why “competition”?

Other varieties are close substitutes. High profits attract entry, while losses cause exit. In long-run equilibrium, entry pushes economic profit toward zero.

Four ingredients: taste for variety, differentiated firms, a fixed cost for every variety, and entry or exit.

Source: Krugman, “Increasing Returns, Monopolistic Competition, and International Trade” (1979).

Plant, stamping dies, engineering, software, model design, crash testing, and certification—paid before the next vehicle is built.

Marginal cost c

Labour time, tires, steel, glass, battery cells, and transport needed for one more vehicle.

Two identical countries can trade without comparative advantage

Suppose Canada and the United States are identical. Each has demand for 600,000 vehicles, and every variety needs 150,000 sales to break even.

Separate markets

Each country supports four varieties. Consumers in either country choose among four.

4varieties available

Integrated market

Canada makes four and the U.S. makes four. Each exports its varieties. Every consumer chooses among eight.

8varieties available

The baseline result and plant rationalization are distinct

Baseline Krugman result

Trade can occur between identical countries. Consumers access more varieties and countries specialize by variety. Entry can leave output per firm unchanged in a symmetric long-run example.

Plant rationalization

When duplicated plants consolidate or firms differ, some producers exit. Surviving plants may lengthen their runs, raising q and reducing F/q.

Limits and evidence: the symmetric model does not say which country gets which variety. The data reveal whether integration appears as more varieties, larger surviving plants, entry or exit, price changes, or some combination.

Observable predictions of the Krugman model

  1. Similar countries can exchange differentiated products within the same industry.
  2. Countries specialize at the variety level even when neither owns the whole industry.
  3. Trade gives consumers access to more varieties. Sticker prices need not fall for this gain to exist.
  4. Market integration can change entry, exit, plant scale, and markups. The exact mix is an empirical question.

Evidence standard: a high GL index is consistent with Krugman trade, but it does not prove the mechanism.

The complete mental model

Krugman in words

Consumers value different vehicle models, while every model requires a fixed investment. A Canadian plant therefore specializes in selected varieties and seeks enough North American sales to cover that fixed cost. Trade lets Canada export those varieties and import others made elsewhere. Consumers obtain more choice. Plant scale can also rise when production rationalizes.

The two analytical bars

A · Separate market size from political size

  • Compare status quo, deeper integration without union, and hypothetical political integration.
  • Distinguish private-market access from shared public institutions.
  • Use the same criteria for all three.
  • Treat the counterfactual neutrally, not as advocacy.

B · Separate population from welfare

  • Compare concentrated and polycentric spatial growth.
  • Distinguish national scale from urban agglomeration.
  • Separate total GDP, productivity per person, and urban welfare.
  • Set measurable housing and infrastructure guardrails.

Both options require a recommendation, the strongest counterargument, a sensitivity check, and an observation that would reverse the verdict.
