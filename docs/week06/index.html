Scale begins with fixed versus variable cost

C(q) = F + cq   ⇒   AC(q) = c + F ÷ q

Fixed cost F

Plant, stamping dies, engineering, software, model design, crash testing, and certification—paid before the next vehicle is built.

Marginal cost c

Labour time, tires, steel, glass, battery cells, and transport needed for one more vehicle.

Economies of scale: more units share the same fixed cost, so fixed cost per unit falls.

Classify A paint shop, one set of tires, a crash test, and one hour of assembly labour.

“Cars” are not one homogeneous good

Consumers and firms value choice

A pickup, minivan, compact car, SUV, delivery van, and luxury crossover perform different jobs even though all are “vehicles.”

More accessible varieties can raise welfare even if total vehicle consumption barely changes.

Producers specialize by variety

  • Canada makes selected models at larger scale.
  • It exports those models.
  • It imports other models made at scale elsewhere.
  • No country must produce every variety.

Explain How can Canada export a Honda CR-V and import another Honda model without contradiction?

Three ingredients generate intra-industry trade

1 · Fixed costs

Larger production runs reduce average cost.

2 · Differentiated varieties

Customers value products that are similar—but not identical.

3 · Specialization

Firms produce a subset of varieties for a larger market.

larger market → fewer duplicated small runs → more output per surviving variety → lower AC + more imported variety

Adjustment is real: some firms, plants, workers, and communities can lose even when aggregate gains are positive.

CUSMA supports scale—but access is conditional

Commitment and scale benefits

  • predictable continental demand;
  • longer production runs and specialized suppliers;
  • common rules and dispute procedures;
  • investment planned around cross-border sales.

Rules and dependence

  • 75% regional value content for light vehicles and core parts;
  • origin compliance can raise input and administrative costs;
  • concentrated market access magnifies U.S. policy shocks;
  • uncertainty can delay fixed investment.

Source: Global Affairs Canada, CUSMA Economic Impact Assessment. Its quantitative results depend on a model and reference scenario.
