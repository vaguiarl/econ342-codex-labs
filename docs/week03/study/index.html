Product space in three ideas

RCA describes a country's export basket. Proximity connects products globally. Density asks how much of a candidate's neighbourhood Canada already occupies.

  1. Compare export shares.
  2. Build product-product links.
  3. Screen candidates—then challenge them.

Ten short slides · about 15 minutes · print-friendly

RCA is a share divided by a benchmark share

RCAc,p = country product share ÷ world product share

RCA ≥ 1: product p is unusually prominent in country c's export basket relative to the world benchmark.

RCA is revealed specialization—not a direct measure of technology or Ricardian opportunity cost.

Proximity is a worldwide product-pair measure

φp,q = min{both ÷ countries with q, both ÷ countries with p}

Screen candidates, then try to disprove them

Density can tell us

  • how related a candidate is to Canada's observed export basket
  • which current specializations contribute most to that relatedness
  • which candidates merit closer investigation

Density cannot establish

  • future demand, profitability, or causal capabilities
  • domestic value added or distributional gains
  • that a subsidy or any other policy passes a cost-benefit test

Audit rule: verify scope and units, calculate one RCA by hand, test proximity and density, reproduce the run, and compare every memo number with the CSV.

Try first—then reveal the answer

1. What is the difference between RCA and Ricardian comparative advantage?

RCA describes how prominent a product is in an observed export basket relative to a benchmark. Ricardian comparative advantage is a model concept based on relative opportunity cost. RCA can reflect technology, resources, geography, policy, prices, firms, and networks.

2. Ten countries specialize in pulp, eight in paper, and six in both. Calculate proximity.

min{6/8, 6/10} = min{0.75, 0.60} = 0.60.

3. What is fixed when calculating Canadian density for coated board?

The country is fixed as Canada and the candidate is fixed as coated board. The neighbouring product q changes as we weight each link by whether Canada has RCA of at least one in that neighbour.

4. Why is a high-density candidate not automatically a recommendation?

The network omits demand, costs, profitability, firms, workers, regulations, geography within Canada, externalities, public cost, and the policy objective.

Ready when you can: calculate and distinguish RCA, proximity, and density—and state one missing fact before calling a candidate realistic.
