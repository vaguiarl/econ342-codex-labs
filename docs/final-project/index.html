Six-page narrative

Make the argument easy to follow.

Use full sentences and paragraphs, US Letter, 11-point body text, one-inch margins, page numbers, and both authors’ names on page 1. Exactly six narrative pages include exhibits and short source notes. Put full references and supporting material in the research ZIP.

Decision and answer

State the question, recommendation, strongest finding, and a condition that could change your answer.

Background and theory

Explain why the problem matters and what the course model predicts in this Canadian setting.

Diagnosis

Use your baseline exhibit to quantify the problem, affected groups, likely constraint, and another explanation.

Proposed answer

Use your second exhibit to compare the recommendation with the strongest feasible alternative.

Evidence, gaps, and risks

Assess how well the answer fits the data, including contrary evidence, remaining gaps, and who bears the risks.

Implementation and added value

Name the actor, first step, timing, success measure, and what your analysis adds to earlier work.

Read Amazon’s explanation of six-page narratives. The course PDF supplies the required format, topic areas, sources, and contribution-agreement template.

Ready to send to Ottawa

A policy analyst should be able to identify the recommendation, trace the key numbers, understand who would gain or lose, and see what they could do next.

A descriptive comparison, conditional scenario, or break-even calculation can earn full marks when the claims fit the evidence. Difference-in-differences is optional. Justify any causal claim.

The updated rubric divides the former ten originality points into five for added value and context and five for the collaboration record. A focused comparison, Canadian application, or synthesis can provide added value when situated in two or three relevant sources or earlier projects.

Shared work, private ratings

Include a jointly updated contribution agreement in the research ZIP. Link roles, milestones, changes, completed tasks, and mutual checks to files or dated versions. Both partners should understand and be able to defend the entire memo.

Each partner emails a private rating of the other partner from 0 to 100, with a brief factual explanation and evidence, to victor_aguiar_lozano@sfu.ca by November 30 at noon. Use the subject ECON342 final project private partner rating - [your name]. Keep ratings out of the shared ZIP and acknowledgment survey.

Use 100 for fully delivering the agreed contribution, a lower score for partial delivery, and 0 for no contribution. Different roles can both represent full collaboration.

The instructor reviews every rating

The instructor determines the verified contribution score and may override any rating upward or downward, even when the two ratings agree. Unsupported or retaliatory ratings are disregarded. Both partners can explain before a disputed or missing rating is resolved or an adjustment is made. A missing rating carries no automatic penalty; zero requires evidence of no work.

Team memo score × verified contribution score ÷ 100
A 90-point memo and an instructor-approved contribution score of 100 give 90 points. A verified score of 80 gives 90 × 0.80 = 72 points.

Full collaboration preserves the full team score. The rubric determines the quality of that shared score.

Presentation and awards

December 1 is regular class.

No in-class project presentation is required. The presentation option is a recorded video, submitted on Canvas.

Optional video: up to five project points

Submit one video of up to ten minutes by Tuesday, December 1 at 11:59 p.m. Vancouver time. Brief a policy audience on your recommendation (2 points), strongest evidence (2 points), and the main trade-off or condition that would change your conclusion (1 point).

To qualify, both partners must speak and independently explain, in their own words, the mechanism, what the evidence shows, and one important limitation. Brief notes are welcome. Speak without reading an AI-generated script.

The bonus is added after the contribution multiplier. A 72-point individual score plus a four-point video bonus gives 76; the maximum is 105/100.

Excellence awards

The highest-ranked team is exempt from sitting the final exam. Each member of the top three teams earns 10 points on their final exam mark out of 100.

Points above 100 on the final exam may be credited to other course assessments. These awards are separate from the optional video bonus.
