A productive cluster can also expose a household to risk

People choose a skill, industry, and place before learning which activities will have a good year.

  1. A productive local industry attracts workers.
  2. A larger cluster can become more productive.
  3. A household values the average reward and the risk of a bad outcome.

In the baseline, workers stay in their chosen industry and place after the shock. Wages adjust. Households have no saving or financial insurance.

Trade opens markets and alternative sources of supply. Its effect on real income risk depends on wages and consumer prices together.

Ma and Ramondo, NBER Working Paper 35942, October 2026.

This paper’s mechanism

A larger industry in one place raises local industry productivity.

Shared skills, suppliers, and knowledge help us picture this external scale effect.

Risk can discourage workers from choosing a cluster in advance. A smaller cluster can lose some productivity benefits of scale.

  • Exhibit 2: historical variability, baseline and reweighted mixes, and sensitivity checks.

Part 2 · Separate workshop

Causal reasoning

What would have happened without the policy or shock?

Differences-in-differences supplies one possible comparison. Its credibility comes from the design and assumptions.

For the final project. This new material will not be tested on Midterm 2.

This workshop is separate from the podcast and from the paper’s model.

The missing counterfactual

A Canadian industry’s employment falls after a trade restriction.

Observed

The industry’s outcome after the restriction.

Missing

The same industry’s outcome at the same time if the restriction had not occurred.

A before-and-after change also includes demand, commodity prices, technology, and the business cycle.

A comparison group can help approximate the missing path. We must explain why its change is informative.

Differences-in-differences with four numbers

Hypothetical employment rates. These are invented groups and outcomes.

GroupBeforeAfterChange
Treated70%64%−6 pp
Comparison60%58%−2 pp

DiD = (64 − 70) − (58 − 60) = −4 pp

If, without the policy, the treated group would also have changed by −2 pp, its counterfactual after rate is 70 − 2 = 68%.

The observed 64% is 4 percentage points below that assumed counterfactual.

Two-group, two-period classroom example. A causal interpretation requires the assumptions on slide 12.

Parallel trends supply the unobserved path

Without treatment, assuming parallel trends:68%. DiD:−4 percentage points.

Invented illustration. The dashed segment imposes the same untreated change as the comparison group. Similar earlier trends support plausibility but cannot prove the missing path. Underlying values.

When the comparison can mislead

Parallel trends: absent treatment, the groups would have had the same average change in the outcome.

  • No anticipation: behaviour should not already respond before the chosen treatment date.
  • No spillovers: treatment should not change the comparison group’s outcome.
  • Other shocks, changing populations, or different measurement can generate different trends.

In trade, supply chains and retaliation can affect both groups. Export exposure may also reflect earlier industrial decline.

Pre-policy plots, institutions, and alternative comparisons help assess the design. A good-looking pre-trend plot alone cannot establish causality.

Roth et al.(2023). The paper’s calibrated welfare comparison is not a DiD estimate.

A clear claim makes the evidence useful

  1. Fix the unit, outcome, policy date, and comparison. Explain the economic channel.
  2. Plot data and check definitions, denominators, and earlier trends.
  3. State whether evidence is descriptive or supports a conditional causal interpretation.
  4. Connect the outcome to household income, prices, assets, and adjustment.
A prompt to copy into Codex

Audit my proposed comparison for [Canadian case]. List the identifying assumption, spillovers, anticipation, and other shocks. Plot pre-policy outcomes. Separate verified facts from assumptions. If the design is weak, explain what a useful descriptive analysis can establish.
